# Introduction

This document describes the design of the Flight Computer (FC) for Firehorn 2. It is intended to provide sufficient detail for a reader to understand the board's operation and reproduce it. The FC is responsible for processing sensor data, managing state transitions, and facilitating communication with other components of the avionics stack. It integrates multiple functions that were previously distributed across separate boards in FH1, including the Navigation Sensor Board (NSB) and the Pyro board. The FC is built around the STM32H743ZIT microcontroller, which provides ample processing power and peripheral interfaces to meet the demands of the mission.

# Functional Description
The FC board can be divided in three functionally different bards. It merges the NSB, the FC and Pyro board from FH1 all onto one board. 

Here is the block diagram presenting an overview of the different functions of the FC:

## Flight Computer
The FC is an STM32H743ZIT that runs the main software in charge of sensor integration, FSM state, and external communication through other means (LoRa, USB, CAN)

![fc_fh2_block_diagram.png](/avionics/fc_fh2_block_diagram.png)

## Power management
The STM32 and all sensors use a 3V3 Vin, and the USB HS PHY needs an 1V8 Vin. Therefor there are 2 LDO's on the board, one for 5V -> 3V3, and one for 5V -> 1V8.

There are 4 possible power inputs:
- 24V Spine
- 5V Spine
- 5V VUSB FS
- 5V VUSB HS

The 24V goes to the pyro channels and therefor is not directly used by the FC.
The three 5V lines need some logic to work together. Before being combined, they are all protected by a polyfuse, a TVS diode, an ideal diode, and decoupling capacitors. 
The two VBUS lines (FS & HS) are ORed after passing through their respective protections to become a single VBUS line. A Power MUX is then used to select the desired power line between the 5V Spine and the VBUS line. The 5V from the Spine is prioritized. 
The output power from the MUX is the clean 5V supply that is then fed in the two LDO's.

## GNSS
The FC is dotted with a GNSS MAX-M10S.

## Storage
Onboard storage is done through an SD card with a 4-bit SDIO communication interface.

## Debugging, flashing and monitoring
The FC is equiped with 2 micro-B UBS ports.
- OTG USB FS (12Mb/s) for DFU -> used to flash the firmware onto the stm32.
- OTG USB HS (480Mb/s) for VCP -> used for communication to a PC once the stm32 is setup.

Additionnaly a basic UART communication header is exposed in case the USB does not suffice.

Lastly, a SWD debugging header is exposed to probe the stm32 for debugging purpouses.

## External communication
The FC sends signals through the Spine for 2 reasons. 
1. Sends RX and TX signals to the RF board somewhere else on the avionics Stack using 2 SPI channels
2. CAN communication to other MCs.

## Communication protocols recap.
Here is a recap of all communication protocols used for the FC for internal and external communication.
Remark: The use of numbers on the communication protocols are the ones used on the actual pinouts. So SPI~x~, with ~x~ being a different number is to show that they are different buses. 
| Function     | Communication Mode       |                     
|--------------|------------------------|
|LoRa Rx| SPI~6~|
|LoRa Tx| SPI~1~|
|TMP and ADX (temp and acc.) | I2C~4~|
| GNSS| UART~7~|
| ICM and BMP| SPI~4~|
| UART header| USUART|
| CAN internet | CAN |
| I2C Mon| I2C over CAN|
| SD card|SDMMC |
| SWD Debug Header| SWD|
|DFU| OTG_USB_FS |
| VCP| OTG_USB_HS|
